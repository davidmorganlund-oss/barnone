Know the rules

Four things to know in Oregon

Voting

Your right to vote comes back when you are released from prison, including on parole or post-prison supervision. You must register again at OregonVotes.gov. A misdemeanor jail sentence never takes away your vote.

State ID

Ask your release counselor about getting your ID, birth certificate, and Social Security card before release. If you leave without an ID, see Oregon DMV for what to bring.

Health coverage

Apply for the Oregon Health Plan right away after release. In October 2025, Oregon dropped its plan to cover adults' health care before release. Limited reentry benefits for people under 21 (or under 26 if they aged out of foster care) begin after release in 2026, with care before release no earlier than 2027.

Clearing a record

Many convictions and arrests can be set aside by filing a motion with the court. Most need a waiting period and a finished sentence, and some serious offenses don't qualify. Free clinics can help you file.
