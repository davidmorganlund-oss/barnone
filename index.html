The Northwest by the numbers

Three neighbors, three very different choices

Idaho, Washington, and Oregon share borders, families, and history. They do not share an approach to incarceration.

Higher than any democracy

Idaho locks up a larger share of its people than any independent democratic country on earth, according to the Prison Policy Initiative.

Idaho's prisons are full

About 10,000 people were in Idaho Department of Correction custody on Jan. 1, 2026, but its prisons have beds for just over 8,200. About 1,750 of them were held in county jails or out of state.

New · Research Brief No. 6

Smart on crime, smart on the budget: Oregon

Close the 160-year-old Oregon State Penitentiary and don't build another prison. Move its staff into units that are built but sitting dark, bring the oldest and sickest home, and put the money into communities instead. Our Washington and Idaho briefs are Nos. 4 and 5.

  1. No. 1

    Similar violent crime, far more prison

    In 2024, Idaho and Utah reported nearly the same violent crime rate. Idaho holds people in state prison at 2.6 times Utah's rate.    Idaho · Nov. 2026
  2. No. 2

    Long and life sentences in Washington

    49% of people in Washington prisons in September 2026 were sentenced to more than 10 years or to life. A 2025 study estimates nearly one in three is serving a long or life sentence with no parole review.    Washington · Nov. 2026
  3. No. 3

    Who would benefit from the Review Act

    Applying the 2025 bill (HB 1125) to the September 2024 prison population, about 3,035 people could ask a judge for a second look. Eligible to ask is not released; DOC's own estimate was 2,188.    Washington · Nov. 2026
  4. No. 4

    Smart on crime, smart on the budget: Washington

    Graduated reentry, clemency, elder parole and unit closures could save Washington about $70 million a year at an accelerated pace.    Washington · Nov. 2026
  5. No. 5

    Smart on crime, smart on the budget: Idaho

    About 10,000 people in custody and beds for 8,200. Eight changes that would spend less and get better results.    Idaho · Nov. 2026
  6. No. 6

    Smart on crime, smart on the budget: Oregon

    Close the Oregon State Penitentiary and don't build another prison. Staff move to mothballed units, the oldest and sickest go home, and more than $1 billion goes to communities instead.    Oregon · Nov. 2026

What we do

Research, policy, and people

Research

Data, findings, briefs

We analyze sentencing, prison, and jail data across three states and publish what we find in plain language, with every number sourced and dated.

Policy

Boise, Olympia, Salem

We brief legislators and agencies, testify on bills that change who goes to prison and for how long, and share our tools so others can do the same.

People

Convenings and reentry

We bring people with lived experience, officials, and advocates into the same room, and we keep a reentry directory for people coming home.

Our approach

We show our work

We study how incarceration is used, what it costs, and whether alternatives can improve safety and accountability. We publish our sources, explain our methods, say what each brief can't show, and keep findings separate from recommendations. You can check our work and still disagree with our conclusions.

We work with lawmakers of every party. We don't hide where we start: the evidence so far suggests the best answers to crime are usually found in communities, in a job, a home, treatment that works, and people who show up. Our research tests that against the Northwest's own numbers.

About BarNone

Founded in Boise in 2019. Working across the Northwest.

BarNone began by welcoming people home from prison in Idaho, with release parties and the Idaho Reentry Summit series. Those years taught us that the hardest problems in reentry start long before release, in the laws that decide who goes to prison and for how long.

Today BarNone, Inc. is a nonpartisan 501(c)(3) research and policy organization studying those decisions across Idaho, Washington, and Oregon.
