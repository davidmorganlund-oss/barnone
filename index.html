The Northwest by the numbers

Three neighbors, three very different choices

Idaho, Washington, and Oregon share borders, families, and history. They do not share an approach to incarceration.

Higher than any democracy

Idaho locks up a larger share of its people than any independent democratic country on earth.

Idaho's prisons are full

About 10,000 people are held in Idaho prisons built for just over 8,200. The overflow goes to county jails and out-of-state beds.

Long and life sentences in Washington

Washington imprisons fewer people per resident than its neighbors, but nearly half of the people in its prisons are serving more than 10 years or life, and most have no way to have their sentence reviewed.

What we do

Research, policy, and people

Research

Data, findings, briefs

We analyze sentencing, prison, and jail data across three states and publish what we find in plain language, with every number sourced and dated.

Policy

Boise, Olympia, Salem

We brief legislators and agencies, testify on bills that change who goes to prison and for how long, and share our tools so others can do the same.

People

Convenings and reentry

We bring people with lived experience, officials, and advocates into the same room, and we keep a reentry directory for people coming home.

Our approach

Evidence over ideology

We follow the data wherever it goes. When a policy keeps people safe at lower cost, we say so. When it fills prisons without making anyone safer, we say that too.

We work with lawmakers of every party. The best answers to crime and incarceration are usually found in communities: a job, a home, treatment that works, and people who show up. Our research asks whether the Northwest is investing in them.

About BarNone

Founded in Boise in 2018. Working across the Northwest.

BarNone began by welcoming people home from prison in Idaho, with release parties and the Idaho Reentry Summit series. Those years taught us that the hardest problems in reentry start long before release, in the laws that decide who goes to prison and for how long.

Today BarNone, Inc. is a nonpartisan 501(c)(3) research and policy organization studying those decisions across Idaho, Washington, and Oregon.
