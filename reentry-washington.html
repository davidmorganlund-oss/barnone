Know the rules

Four things to know in Washington

Voting

Your right to vote comes back automatically as soon as you are no longer serving a sentence of total confinement in prison. You can register the day you get out at VoteWA.gov.

State ID

Since January 2025, the Department of Corrections must help people held 60 days or more get a state ID before release, and it pays the fee. The photo does not have to show you were incarcerated. This does not cover city or county jails.

Health coverage

Apple Health (Medicaid) can now start up to 90 days before release in participating prisons and jails. It covers case management, support for substance use treatment, and a 30-day supply of medication when you leave. Ask your counselor whether your facility takes part.

Clearing a record

Many convictions can be vacated after a waiting period. If you had a drug possession conviction, the State v. Blake decision may let you vacate it and get back fines you paid.
